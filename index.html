HydroGuard: Smart Water Quality Bottle

A portable bottle-format tester that reads water parameters, judges them against WHO limits and explains the result in plain language.

Problem / objective

Lab tests are slow and costly, while handheld kits measure only one or two values and need expert reading. The goal was a low-cost, portable device that combines several sensors, shows an instant SAFE / NOT SAFE verdict and gives non-experts a clear explanation. It supports UN SDG 3, 6 and 9.

My role & contributions

  • Co-developed the project in a three-person team supervised by Dr. Mohammed Khairi Ishak.
  • Contributed to sensor integration, firmware, and technical documentation (Project I theory report and Project II implementation report).

Technologies & tools

ESP32Arduino IDE / C++pH · TDS · Turbidity · DS18B20I²C OLED (SSD1306)On-device web serverOpenAI APIArduinoJsonTP4056 battery charging

Key results & learnings

  • 4 sensors read concurrently with no cross-interference or ADC instability.
  • Sensor response in 1–2 s; AI safety summaries refreshed every 2 minutes.
  • Validated across tap, filtered, bottled and mixed-contaminant samples; pH checked against three buffer solutions, temperature against a reference thermometer.
  • All six target specs met, including WHO-based classification and a router-free local dashboard.
  • Learned to manage analog noise, ADC non-linearity and temperature compensation on low-cost hardware.

Smart Vertical Farm

A microcontroller-based controller that automates cooling, lighting and watering for an indoor, stacked-layer farm.

Problem / objective

Traditional farming is limited by land and unpredictable weather. Vertical farming grows crops indoors in layers, but it needs constant control of light, water and temperature. The goal was a low-cost “automatic gardener” that handles these conditions on a schedule.

My role & contributions

  • Member of a three-person team (Ajman University, Embedded Systems course, instructors Dr. Khalid S. Ammar and Eng. Ahmed Osman).
  • Worked on hardware prototyping, firmware logic and the written project report.
  • Add your specific tasks here.

Technologies & tools

PIC16F887Embedded CDS3231 real-time clockI²CLCD 20×4 displayPICkit 2 programmerMB102 power moduleBreadboard prototyping

Key results & learnings

  • Temperature control: fan switches on above 24 °C and off below 18 °C, using the DS3231’s built-in temperature reading.
  • Lighting: grow lights run on a day/night schedule, on between 6 PM and 6 AM.
  • Watering: an output pulses every 5 minutes to trigger irrigation.
  • Live time, date and temperature shown on the LCD; coin-cell backup keeps the clock accurate through power loss.
  • Learned to work around real constraints: budget, sensor availability, substituting parts, and a tight 4-week schedule.
